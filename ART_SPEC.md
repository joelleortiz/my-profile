# Art spec

Everything needed to redraw any sprite of the study scene in Aseprite and drop it in without touching code.

## The canvas

- The world is **880 × 480** scene pixels. The page scales it by whole numbers only (2×, 3× … device pixels per scene pixel) with nearest-neighbour sampling, so draw at 1:1 and never anti-alias against transparency.
- One camera looks squarely at the back wall from 5 m away at eye height (125 cm). The desk, laptop, chair and room are projected from it, so they share one viewpoint, and the back wall is flat to the viewer, so lettering on it stays square. The desk set (desk, chair, laptop, Joelle) is turned 22° toward the viewer's left. `scripts/art/sprites/study.ts` holds the camera and builds every projected sprite from it.
- Joelle is drawn at about 2.2 px per cm: the head is 54 × 62 px with hair, the face 30 px wide and the shoulders about 80 px across, with the hair ending on the shoulders. `me` has 34 rows of headroom above the head for the stretch. Myles sitting is 72 px tall; Margot curled asleep is 56 × 23, with room in her sprite to stand and stretch along the sill.
- Light comes from the window, on the left. Shade surfaces facing away from it with the `d` and `d2` shades.
- Sprite positions are whole pixels. The parallax offset of the view outside the window is rounded to whole pixels too.

## Layers and parallax

Back to front. The room never moves. Only the view outside the window (`far`) shifts with the pointer, or drifts slowly on touch screens, by up to 4 px across and 2 px down, as if seen through the glass. With reduced motion it stays still.

| Layer  | Contents                                                                                                                                    |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `far`  | Sky gradient, sun, moon and stars (drawn by code), `clouds`, `city`. Shown only through the window glass, and the only layer that moves.    |
| `wall` | `room`, `window`, `bookcase`, `plant`, `sign`, `corkboard`, `clock` (its hands are drawn by code), `swatch`, `margot` (on the sill), `zzz`. |
| `near` | `chair`, `me-legs`, `me`, `me-face`, `desk`, `me-arms`, `laptop`, the stickers, `radio`, `lamp`, `coffee`, `myles`, `hearts`.               |

`room` covers the whole world (880 × 480) and it is opaque everywhere. The window glass is cut out of it by the scene file, and the sky is drawn only inside that cut. So that the largest shift never shows an edge, `city` reaches at least 4 px past each side of the glass and 2 px below it, and the clouds stay clear of their frame's edges.

## The wall

`room` is the room shell seen from the camera: the back wall with a crown moulding and a skirting board (`frame` shades), the side walls a shade darker, the floorboards and the ceiling (`wall.d2`). The shadow under the desk is baked into it.

The back wall is papered: `wall.b` with a tiny four-dot flower in `wall.l` on a 12 px diamond lattice (one flower every 12 px, rows offset by 6), counted from the world's origin so it tiles seamlessly. It is faint on purpose, and as a wall material it takes every palette, the time-of-day tint and the lights. A redrawn `room` should keep a pattern this quiet so nothing on the wall loses contrast.

## The key palette

Every sprite is drawn in the **key palette**: 40 materials with four shades each, ordered dark to light: `d2` (deep shadow), `d` (shadow), `b` (base), `l` (highlight). At runtime each key colour is replaced by the same material and shade from the active palette, so one drawing works in all four palettes.

- Load `src/lib/scene/art/key-palette.gpl` in Aseprite (Palette → Load palette) and draw only with those colours. Index 0 is transparent.
- The fixed colour `white` (sticker die-cut borders, glints) never changes with the palette. The paint-swatch card's chips are ordinary materials (`sweater`, `sticker1`, `sticker0`, `lamp`), so they show the current palette.
- Materials added for the study: `hoodie`, `jeans`, `cork`, `wood` (frames), `paper` (the sign and notes) and `radio`. They come after the original 34, so older indices did not move.
- `lampGlow` and `screen` give off light: after dark the tint leaves them alone, so bulbs, the lit lamp and the radio dial stay bright.
- Any colour that is not in the key palette passes through unchanged, which is almost never what you want.
- The key palette is frozen. `scripts/art/key-palette.ts` will not overwrite it unless run with `--force`. New materials and fixed colours go at the end of their lists, and a fixed colour must not equal an existing key colour, so regenerating leaves every existing key colour where it was.
- Each palette's ramps come from its base colours in `src/lib/scene/palettes.ts`: `d2` moves up to 26° toward the palette's shadow hue (saturation +0.06, lightness −0.27), `d` up to 13° (+0.03, −0.13), `l` up to 10° toward its light hue (−0.02, +0.10). Near-greys take the shadow and light hues with low saturation. Retro 32 then snaps every shade to Endesga 32 and dithers the finished frame. A palette can also set a material's four shades outright (`ramps`): Retro 32 does for the tabby, a silver body with brown stripes, since Endesga 32 has no brown-grey.

## Sprite sheets

Each sprite is a PNG sheet plus Aseprite JSON in `src/lib/scene/art/`, named after the sprite (`me.png`, `me.json`). The loader reads everything from the JSON; no frame coordinates live in the code.

Export from Aseprite with **File → Export Sprite Sheet**:

- Layout: any (by rows, horizontal strip or packed). Trim, padding and **merge duplicates** are all fine; trimmed frames are placed using `spriteSourceSize`.
- Output: JSON data, **Array** or **Hash**, with **Tags** and **Slices** ticked. Keep the image indexed with the key palette.
- Keep the sprite's name, its tags and its slices exactly as listed below.

The sheets in the repo are generated by `npm run art` from `scripts/art/sprites/*.ts`: `study.ts` places and projects the study scene, and `parts.ts` draws Joelle, the cats and the props. Identical frames share one cell in the sheet. Once a sprite is replaced with a hand-drawn Aseprite export, remove it from the list in `scripts/art/sprites/index.ts` so the build stops overwriting it. `npm run art` also refreshes the tables in this file, and `node scripts/art/study-positions.ts` prints where the projected sprites sit, for `scene.json`.

### Anchors and slices

- **Anchor:** the pivot of the slice named `anchor` (in Aseprite: the slice's pivot point). Without a pivot, the bottom centre of that slice; without the slice, the bottom centre of the frame. `at` in `scene.json` is where the anchor goes, in scene pixels.
- **Slices** are rectangles in frame pixels. A slice can have a key per frame; an empty key (0 × 0) means "not in this frame".
  - `hit`: the clickable area of `myles`, `margot`, `coffee` and the stickers that are links (`sticker-github`, `sticker-name` for LinkedIn and `sticker-scroll` for the CV). In scene pixels it is the sprite's top left plus the slice. The other stickers are plain art, with no slice.
  - `face` (on `me`): where `me-face` is drawn, so blinks and the screen glint follow the head. It has a key per frame; the key is empty during the glance at the window, which hides the overlay.
  - `hearts` (on `myles`) and `zzz` (on `margot`): where the floating hearts and z's start. Joelle's hand on his head sits just below `hearts`.

### Tags and events

Tags name the actions. Frame durations come from the sheet. A tag's **user data** lists events fired when a frame of that tag starts, as `frame:event` pairs counted from the tag's first frame, for example `0:type`. The scene uses them for sounds: `type`, `pet-start`, `pet-end`, `sip`, `cup-down`, `margot-wake` and `margot-stretch`.

Joelle and both cats are animated.

## The scene file

`src/lib/scene/art/scene.json` places everything.

**Layouts.** The wall objects sit differently on a desktop (`landscape`, the sign stacked above the corkboard), a phone (`portrait`) and a phone held sideways (`short`); the desk set is the same in all three except the `radio`, which stands at the laptop's front corner in `portrait`, where only the middle of the desk is in view, and on the left of the desk elsewhere. Each layout has a `focus` (what must be in the first view) and `places`: boxes on the back wall for the `window` glass, the `sign`, the corkboard (`cork`), the `clock`, the `swatch` card and the decor (`bookcase`, `plant`), and the `radio`'s spot on the desk. A layout may leave a place out, and then that object isn't drawn there.

The `draw` list is the drawing order, back to front:

- `{ "sprite", "layer", "at" }` draws a sprite with its anchor at `at`, or `"place"` (plus an optional `"offset"`) to put the anchor on a place in the current layout. Optional: `tag` (the tag to play), `dark` (a tag dissolved in after dark), `weight` (dissolve the whole sprite by `day`, `dark`, `night` or `stars`), `attach: [sprite, slice]` (place the anchor at another sprite's slice), `follow` (show the frame another sprite with the same frames is on: `me-arms` follows `me`) and `requires` (drawn only while a flag is on, or with a leading `!` while it's off: `cv` for the scroll sticker until the CV page exists, `hearts` while Myles is petted, `asleep` for Margot's z's, `sound` and `!sound` for the radio).
- `{ "cut" }` clears a place's box from what is drawn so far; the window glass is cut so the sky shows through it, and nowhere else.
- `{ "procedural": "clockHands" }` draws the wall clock's hands over the `clock` sprite, so the sprite is only the face. `clock` in the scene file gives the centre on the sprite and the hand lengths. The hands show the visitor's time, with a second hand, or a fixed time when the visitor picks a time of day.
- `{ "light" }` screens a light over everything drawn so far, masked to opaque pixels. Lights are defined under `lights`: a `glow` ellipse, a `shaft` polygon (relative to a place when it names one) or a `pool`, which lights a place's box evenly (the picture light on the sign, the fairy lights on the corkboard). Sprites drawn after a light are not lit by it; Myles comes after the lamp so its dithering never lands on him.
- `{ "procedural" }` draws the sky, stars or sun and moon. They are laid out on the window glass of the current layout: `sky.orbs` and `sky.stars` give positions as shares of the glass.

The clock and the swatch card are the time-of-day and palette switchers, and their clickable boxes are their places. `insets` gives boxes relative to a place: the sign's lettering and each corkboard note (the text there is HTML), and the radio's clickable box around its anchor (the sound toggle).

Sky colours, the ambient tint and light strengths per time of day come from the palette and `src/lib/scene/lighting.ts`.

## Fit and crop

Without JavaScript the page shows `static/scene-dusk.png`, the whole world at dusk in Cool & moody, with the sign and corkboard text laid over their boards. The Open Graph image `static/og.png` is the page at 2× framed on the whole desk scene, from the clock to the sign. After changing the art, capture both again with `node scripts/still/capture.mjs` while the dev server runs. The site icons (Myles's head at 16, 32 and 180 px) are drawn in `scripts/art/icons.ts` and written by `npm run art`.

The page picks a layout and a whole-number scale for each viewport (`src/lib/scene/fit-view.js`). The scale is the largest at which a layout's `focus` fits, comparing the desktop and phone layouts. It is never below 2 CSS px per scene pixel: when neither fits at that, a phone held sideways uses `short`, and anything smaller crops the room around the text and switchers. The view never runs past the world. Lettering on the sign and notes grows with the scale up to the 3× size; above that the objects get more room around the text.

Keep anything important inside the focus of each layout:

| Layout      | Focus (x, y, w × h) |
| ----------- | ------------------- |
| `landscape` | 220, 120, 478 × 240 |
| `portrait`  | 351, 32, 180 × 328  |
| `short`     | 248, 172, 358 × 180 |
